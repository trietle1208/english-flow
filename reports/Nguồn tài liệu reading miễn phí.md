# Four licenses unlock commercial reading

EnglishFlow can legally seed a commercial, self-hosted reading feature from **three passage pools plus original work**: **VOA Learning English originals** (public-domain U.S. government text with requested credit), **StoryWeaver / Book Dash book text** under **CC BY 4.0**, and **Project Gutenberg / Standard Ebooks works that are unrestricted in both the United States and Vietnam**. Comprehension questions, CEFR tags, and Vietnamese support must be written or human-revised in-house — no official CEFR-tagged commercial reader dump exists, and the best-looking ESL sites (Newsela, British Council, Breaking News English, ESL Fast, RACE) forbid commercial redistribution. Grammar’s CEFR-J + Tatoeba + TALPCo stack does **not** transfer as passages: those sources stay taxonomy and sentence glosses. Wikipedia / SQuAD-family items are commercially allowed but **ShareAlike** infects adapted lessons. Taxonomy below matches `docs/research/grammar-dataset-license-research.md`: `PRODUCTION_ALLOWED` (commercial, no required attribution), `ATTRIBUTION_REQUIRED` (commercial if cite / CC-BY / Apache notice), `RESEARCH_ONLY` (NC / research-educational), `UNKNOWN` (no readable LICENSE or contradiction), `DO_NOT_USE` (commercial ban or third-party copyright too high). Date: **September 2026**.

## No official CEFR reader dump is commercially open

The product need is passages at **A1–C1**, comprehension items, and optional Vietnamese support. What the open web actually offers is the inverse: large **children’s picture-book** catalogs with informal reading levels, adult **news and encyclopedia** text with no CEFR field, and research corpora whose expert CEFR labels sit on **non-commercial** licenses. CEFR-J — already in EnglishFlow’s grammar `content_sources` — publishes a wordlist and grammar profile that “can be used for research and commercial purposes with no charge, provided that you cite the dataset properly,” plus CAN-DO **reading test** PDFs that require citation for education, research, or commercial use. It does **not** publish a reading-passage corpus; the official “CEFR-J Text Profile” line still reads that related materials will be uploaded later, and the shipped tool is **CVLA**, a level *estimator* that the site itself warns is unstable below about **300 words** ([CEFR-J download](https://www.cefr-j.org/download.html); [OLP CEFR-J terms](https://github.com/openlanguageprofiles/olp-en-cefrj)).

StoryWeaver Levels 1–4 are word-range literacy stages (Level 1 up to 50 words; Level 3 **600–1,500**), not CEFR ([StoryWeaver reading levels](https://storyweaver.org.in/en/reading-levels)). African Storybook’s own Play listing says its Levels 1–5 “are **not graded readers**” ([ASb Reader](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?hl=en_US&id=org.saide.ASbReader)). VOA Learning English is written for learners and ships matching MP3s, but its official reuse page never assigns CEFR ([VOA Request Our Content](https://learningenglish.voanews.com/p/6861.html)). ESL Fast states that its six site levels “**do not correspond exactly to the six CEFR levels**” ([ESL Fast About](https://www.eslfast.com/about-eslfast.htm)). Newsela is Lexile / U.S. grade, not CEFR, and is gated to academic NDA research ([Newsela corpus access](https://newsela.com/legal/data); [Xu et al., TACL](https://doi.org/10.1162/tacl_a_00139)). The only large expert CEFR **sentence** set in this inventory, CEFR-SP, is **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** and mixes Wiki-Auto with Newsela-Auto and SCoRE ([Arase et al. 2022](https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.416.pdf); [UniversalCEFR/cefr_sp_en](https://huggingface.co/datasets/UniversalCEFR/cefr_sp_en)).

Open commercial-friendly storybooks ship **passages and illustrations only**. The OneStopEnglish *research* dump is **189 texts × 3 levels = 567** articles with no question key ([OneStopEnglishCorpus README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nishkalavallabhi/OneStopEnglishCorpus/master/README.md)). CLEAR is excerpts plus readability scores under a GitHub **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** README ([CLEAR-Corpus README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/scrosseye/CLEAR-Corpus/main/README.md)). The sites that *do* bundle article-plus-exercises — Breaking News English, ESL Fast, British Council LearnEnglish, CommonLit assessments — are the ones that ban apps. Production quizzes are therefore an EnglishFlow original, the same way grammar MCC items were kept off Zanichelli.

## VOA, StoryWeaver, and dual-jurisdiction PD texts clear commercial seed

**VOA Learning English originals** are the cleanest adult learner-news source. Official terms state that Learning English texts, MP3s, photos, and videos “are in the public domain” and may be reprinted “for educational and commercial purposes, **with credit to learningenglish.voanews.com**,” while AP, Reuters, and AFP material “are copyrighted, so you are not allowed to republish them” ([VOA Request Our Content](https://learningenglish.voanews.com/p/6861.html)). The same exclusive-VOA public-domain + credit + wire ban appears on VOA Learning English terms and on **VOA Tiếng Việt** ([VOA LE terms](https://learningenglish.voanews.com/p/6021.html); [VOA Tiếng Việt copyright](https://www.voatiengviet.com/p/3943.html)). EnglishFlow maps “commercial + required credit” to **`ATTRIBUTION_REQUIRED`**, matching CEFR-J. The two VOA language services are **not** a ready-made aligned EN–VI news corpus; pairing same-event stories is product work, and every article still needs a human wire-scrub. Do not use VOA marks as product branding.

**StoryWeaver** (Pratham) and **Book Dash** are the cleanest A1–A2 extensive-reading stems. StoryWeaver’s official attributions page and FAQs license **all stories and images CC BY 4.0**, allowing remix and commercial use if credit goes to the original creators, StoryWeaver, the publisher, and the donor; **read-alongs and videos are CC BY-NC-ND 4.0** and stay off production ([StoryWeaver Attributions](https://storyweaver.org.in/en/attributions); [StoryWeaver FAQs](https://storyweaver.org.in/en/help/faqs)). Book Dash’s reuse guide is equally explicit: republish on apps, adapt, translate, print and sell, with credit to the four creatives, a link to bookdash.org, a visible logo, and a statement of any adaptation — library size **200+ titles** ([Book Dash reuse](https://bookdash.org/re-using-the-book-dash-content/)). African Storybook and the Global Digital Library are usable **per title** only: GDL’s print-repo README said at 2020 launch that “more than 90% of the titles allow commercial re-use,” with some **CC BY-NC-SA** leftovers ([GDL license](https://digitallibrary.io/about/license/); [GDL print README](https://github.com/GlobalDigitalLibraryio/print)). Bloom Library has no blanket commercial grant; default site contributions are **CC BY-NC 4.0** ([Bloom Terms](https://bloom.sil.org/terms)).

**Project Gutenberg** and **Standard Ebooks** unlock B2–C1 literary passages **after a dual-jurisdiction filter**. Gutenberg’s permission page allows any commercial use of texts “not restricted by U.S. copyright law”; if the Project Gutenberg trademark and license header are stripped, the remaining text is unrestricted by U.S. IP. Attribution to PG is optional. Copyrighted PG items are marked in the ebook header and stay `DO_NOT_USE`. Charging while keeping the PG name triggers verbatim-copy rules and **20% of gross profits** as trademark royalties ([PG permission](https://www.gutenberg.org/policy/permission.html); [PG license](https://www.gutenberg.org/policy/license.html)). Standard Ebooks dedicates its editorial layer to the public domain via **CC0 1.0** while warning that users outside the U.S. “must check their local laws”; the collection is generally works published before **1 January 1931** ([SE About](https://standardebooks.org/about); [SE Uncopyright example](https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/charles-dickens/a-christmas-carol/text/uncopyright)). Vietnam’s Luật Sở hữu trí tuệ Điều 27 protects most literary works for the author’s life **plus 50 years**, expiring at 24:00 on 31 December of the expiry year ([VN IP Law Art. 27](https://thuvienphapluat.vn/van-ban/EN/So-huu-tri-tue/Law-No-50-2005-QH11-of-November-29-2005-on-Intellectual-property/84542/tieng-anh.aspx)). A U.S.-unrestricted 1925 novel by an author who died in 1980 remains protected in Vietnam until **31 December 2030**. Operational filter for a 2026 Vietnam-hosted product: US-unrestricted header **and** author death year **≤ 1975**. Prefer short stories and children’s titles over 80k-word novels; slice 300–800 words at chapter boundaries; assign CEFR in-house. **LibriVox** audio of those same cleared works is public domain for commercial use; official transcripts do not exist — pair audio with the Gutenberg/SE text, never with a third-party “LibriVox transcript” dump ([LibriVox About](https://librivox.org/pages/about-librivox/)).

**NASA / U.S. federal employee text** is not copyrightable in the U.S. under 17 U.S.C. § 105(a); NASA asks to be acknowledged and forbids using insignia or implying endorsement, and third-party captions convey no sublicense ([17 U.S.C. § 105](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A17+section%3A105+edition%3Aprelim%29); [NASA images and media](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/)). Treat original NASA explainers as **`ATTRIBUTION_REQUIRED`** science reading at B2–C1 after a media scrub.

Wikimedia text — English Wikipedia, Simple English Wikipedia, Wikibooks, **Wikivoyage** — is commercially reusable under **CC BY-SA 4.0** (reusers may also choose GFDL). Foundation Terms require attribution (a URL to the article is enough), a license notice, indication of changes, and **ShareAlike on modifications** ([WMF Terms of Use §7](https://foundation.wikimedia.org/wiki/Policy:Terms_of_Use); [Wikivoyage copyleft](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Wikivoyage:CC-by-sa)). Simple Wikipedia’s local copyrights page still describes GFDL-only text and is stale relative to the Foundation ToU; production should follow the live page banner plus Foundation §7, not the outdated local policy ([Simple:Copyrights](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Copyrights)). Fair-use media on Wikipedia is **not** a grant. Putting a Wikipedia or Wikivoyage passage into EnglishFlow and simplifying it, or adding a Vietnamese translation of that expression, likely makes the **adapted package** CC BY-SA 4.0. That is compatible with a commercial self-hosted app *displaying* attributed text; it is incompatible with treating the adapted lesson JSON as proprietary EnglishFlow-only content. Keep SA passages in a labeled `content_sources` lane, separate from original theory and original quizzes.

True **CC BY** (no SA, no NC, no ND) news is scarce. **LSE Blogs** are CC BY 4.0 with commercial adaptation and TASL attribution unless a post says otherwise ([LSE republishing](https://blogs.lse.ac.uk/republishing-policy/)). **Our World in Data** writing they produced is CC BY, but their FAQ forbids material edits without prior approval — verbatim explainers are `ATTRIBUTION_REQUIRED`; CEFR rewrites stay `UNKNOWN` until written OK ([OWID how-to](https://ourworldindata.org/how-to-use-our-world-in-data/)). **The Conversation** is **CC BY-ND**; the UK edition may charge a commercial fee — `DO_NOT_USE` for leveling ([US republishing](https://theconversation.com/us/republishing-guidelines); [UK republishing](https://gcp.theconversation.com/uk/republishing-guidelines)).

On the QA side, no surveyed MRC dataset is `PRODUCTION_ALLOWED`. A Wikipedia- or Gutenberg-backed cluster is `ATTRIBUTION_REQUIRED` for **both** passage and questions: **SQuAD 1.1 / 2.0** and **XQuAD** (CC BY-SA 4.0; XQuAD is 240 paragraphs / 1,190 pairs including professional **Vietnamese**), **BoolQ** (CC BY-SA 3.0), **HotpotQA** (CC BY-SA 4.0), **Belebele eval** only (CC BY-SA 4.0 on FLORES-200; 900 four-way MCQs per language including `eng_Latn` and `vie_Latn`; the assembled **train** set is majority RACE / NC), **FairytaleQA** (Apache-2.0 questions on 278 Gutenberg children’s stories / 10,580 items — still needs per-title US+VN PD check), plus TyDi QA and Natural Questions under Apache 2.0 with Wikipedia SA still attaching to the passage layer ([SQuAD explorer](https://rajpurkar.github.io/SQuAD-explorer/); [XQuAD README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-deepmind/xquad/master/README.md); [BoolQ README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research-datasets/boolean-questions/master/README.md); [Belebele README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/belebele/main/README.md); [FairytaleQA](https://github.com/uci-soe/fairytaleqadata)). None of these carry official CEFR. They are optional later seed, not the MVP passage ladder.

## Exam, news, and ESL classroom sites fail redistribution

“Free to read in a browser” is not a production license. **Newsela** limits platform use to “personal, educational, or other non-commercial use” and releases its research corpus only to academically affiliated researchers under NDA, last updated September 2025 ([Newsela Terms](https://solutions.newsela.com/legal/terms); [Newsela corpus access](https://newsela.com/legal/data)). **British Council** LearnEnglish app terms forbid copying or commercially exploiting British Council content; TeachingEnglish states materials “cannot be redistributed or used for commercial purposes”; organisation terms (National Archives capture) allow personal non-commercial copy with credit and bar republishing on another website ([LearnEnglish apps ToU](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/terms-of-use-for-apps); [TeachingEnglish copyright](https://www.teachingenglish.org.uk/copyright); [archived BC terms](https://webarchive.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ukgwa/20250418110908mp_/https:/www.britishcouncil.org/terms)). **BBC Learning English** and **BBC Vietnamese** allow personal / classroom non-commercial use only; teachers “may not make any charge” and “may not make the material available through any other website”; global BBC terms also call out AI/ML training as not non-commercial ([BBC LE terms](https://www.bbc.co.uk/worldservice/learningenglish/terms.shtml); [BBC Vietnamese terms](https://www.bbc.com/vietnamese/institutional-37622991); [BBC Global ToU](https://www.bbc.com/pages/terms-of-use)). **News in Levels** forbids copying except personal student use or small live classes; the February 2026 operator PDF bars compiling texts into a digital coursebook ([News in Levels conditions](https://newsinlevels.com/conditions-of-use/); [English in Levels 2026 PDF](https://www.englishinlevels.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Terms_and_Conditions_English_in_Levels.pdf)).

**Breaking News English** is explicit: “NONE OF THE MATERIALS ON THIS WEBSITE CAN BE SOLD OR MONETIZED IN ANY FORM,” and reproduction of article or exercises on any **app**, LMS, or book is not granted ([BNE copyright](https://breakingnewsenglish.com/copyright.html)). **ESL Fast / rong-chang.com** owns the content and forbids hosting elsewhere or inclusion in “any commercial publications or applications, online or offline,” without written consent ([rong-chang About](https://www.rong-chang.com/about.htm)). **wikiHow** ended CC licensing for new user content on **24 March 2025**; staff text was never CC; pre-2025 community steps remain **CC BY-NC-SA 3.0**, and NC includes an app with ads or a paid app ([wikiHow:Creative Commons](https://www.wikihow.com/wikiHow:Creative-Commons)). **CommonLit** original articles and questions are **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**; third-party library texts may not be stored off-site; Free Reading Assessment / Assessment Series “are not CC”; uploading materials to AI tools is forbidden ([CommonLit Support art. 451](https://support.commonlit.org/article/451-i-want-to-respect-copyright-and-permission-rules-what-am-i-allowed-to-do-with-commonlit-materials-and-resources-can-i-download-and-print-articles-can-i-share-them-what-about-assessments)). Macmillan **OneStopEnglish website** is a personal/pedagogical license with no title transfer ([Macmillan English ToU](https://www.macmillanenglish.com/by/terms-of-use)). The 2018 **OneStopEnglishCorpus** on GitHub/Zenodo is a separate compiler release under **CC BY-SA 4.0** ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nishkalavallabhi/OneStopEnglishCorpus/master/README.md); [ACL W18-0535](https://aclanthology.org/W18-0535/)), but the articles are teacher rewrites of **The Guardian** for Macmillan’s site; Guardian/Macmillan copyright of the underlying news is not independently waived on those pages. Treat the *website* as `DO_NOT_USE` (do not scrape) and the *corpus* as **`UNKNOWN` for production seed** until counsel reviews the Guardian layer — do not put it in the MVP.

Exam-derived QA is the other hard stop. **RACE** (≈28,000 passages / ≈100,000 questions from Chinese middle- and high-school English exams) is “available for non-commercial research purpose only” and forbids commercial exploit of “any portion of the contexts **and any portion of derived data**” ([CMU RACE](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~glai1/data/race/)). **ReClor** repeats that formula for graduate-admission exam items; the unzip password is literally `for_non-commercial_research_purpose_only` ([ReClor](https://whyu.me/reclor/)). There is **no questions-only commercial carve-out**: splitting stems from passages does not create a license. **DREAM**’s entire `license.txt` is “intended for non-commercial research purpose only” ([DREAM license](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nlpdata/dream/master/license.txt)). **NewsQA**’s MIT file covers **code only**; CNN “does not waive any rights of ownership in its articles” ([Maluuba/newsqa](https://github.com/Maluuba/newsqa)). **MultiRC** is a UIUC research/academic license that “would [not] permit Licensee to incorporate the Software into a commercial product” ([CogComp/multirc LICENSE](https://github.com/CogComp/multirc/blob/master/LICENSE)). **SciQ** is CC BY-NC 3.0 ([allenai/sciq](https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/sciq)). **Belebele’s assembled train set** is majority RACE and “licensed under CC-BY-NC” ([Belebele README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/belebele/main/README.md)). **CoQA** is mixed: Wikipedia + literature slices are CC BY-SA 4.0; the exam domain inherits RACE ([CoQA](https://stanfordnlp.github.io/coqa/)). **NarrativeQA** Apache grant covers questions, answers, and Wikipedia summaries — **not** the copyrighted novels the download script fetches ([NarrativeQA README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-deepmind/narrativeqa/master/README.md)).

Hugging Face and Kaggle cards do not override owner terms. The Kaggle “CEFR Levelled English Texts” upload is marked CC0 while admitting texts from British Council, ESL Fast, and CNN/DailyMail — `DO_NOT_USE` ([Kaggle CEFR texts](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/amontgomerie/cefr-levelled-english-texts)). NewsQA’s HF `mit` tag is the code license mis-applied to the dataset card.

## Vietnamese support is human work, not PhoMT

Grammar already cleared **Tatoeba text (CC BY 2.0 FR)** and **TALPCo (CC BY 4.0)** as `ATTRIBUTION_REQUIRED`. Both remain **sentence-scale**: Tatoeba Terms license contributions as sentences, not continuous passages; TALPCo is `Sentence_ID` plus one line per language with a Japanese pivot ([Tatoeba ToU v1](https://en.wiki.tatoeba.org/articles/show/terms-of-use-v1); [TALPCo README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/matbahasa/TALPCo/master/readme.md)). Reuse them as glosses under a reading passage — `sentence_vi` — the same way grammar examples already do. **Wiktionary** definitions are CC BY-SA 4.0 + GFDL; quotations from living authors stay third-party ([Wiktionary:Copyrights](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Wiktionary:Copyrights)). **WikiMatrix** EN–VI is mined Wikipedia sentences under CC BY-SA, not article-aligned readers ([OPUS WikiMatrix](https://opus.nlpl.eu/legacy/WikiMatrix.php)). Simple English Wikipedia and Vietnamese Wikipedia are independently written encyclopedias connected by interlanguage links, not a translation pair.

The large EN–VI bitexts that look like “free bilingual reading” fail the commercial test for the same reasons grammar already recorded. **PhoMT** is research/educational only and forbids redistribution ([PhoMT README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/VinAIResearch/PhoMT/master/README.md)). **MTet** is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 and mixes TED, movie subtitles, and fictional books ([MTet HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/albertvillanova/mtet/raw/main/README.md)). **TED** talks are CC BY-NC-ND 4.0; NC includes “an app of any kind,” and ND bars unauthorized translations; a “Subscription-Based Educational Service” needs a paid TED license ([TED Talks Usage Policy](https://www.ted.com/about/our-organization/our-policies-terms/ted-talks-usage-policy)). **EVBCorpus** is structurally a bilingual reader corpus (15 books, 5,000 news, 2,000 subtitles — 2.29M pairs) with **no LICENSE file** and an email-the-maintainer README ([EVBCorpus README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/qhungngo/EVBCorpus/master/README.md)) — still `UNKNOWN`. **OpenSubtitles** is film/TV dialogue OPUS believes it may redistribute, not a commercial grant from rightsholders.

No official Bộ GDĐT English reading corpus with a commercial-open LICENSE was found. “Tiếng Anh Global Success” / “Kết nối tri thức” school PDFs that “các đơn vị, trường học… có thể tải về miễn phí để sử dụng” are a school-supply instruction, not a sublicense to seed a commercial app. Cambridge’s partnership on Vietnamese English textbooks is commercial publishing ([VnExpress](https://vnexpress.net/nxb-dh-cambridge-bien-soan-sach-giao-khoa-tieng-anh-tai-viet-nam-4435635.html)). The 2016 e-learning contest required **CC BY / CC BY-SA** on submitted *lesson designs*, not on national SGK.

You **may** machine-translate a truly public-domain English passage into Vietnamese without infringing the expired English copyright. You **may not** label unedited MT as EnglishFlow original IP. U.S. Copyright Office Circular 14 treats a translation of a PD work as a copyrightable derivative only in the *new* expression; Compendium §709.1 states a translation “performed by a machine without human intervention cannot be registered” ([Circular 14](https://copyright.gov/circs/circ14.pdf); [Compendium ch. 700](https://www.copyright.gov/comp3/chap700/ch700-literary-works.pdf)). Vietnamese Luật SHTT Điều 4(8) defines translation as a derivative work; Điều 14(3) requires the author’s own intellectual labor ([155/VBHN-VPQH 2025](https://thuvienphapluat.vn/van-ban/So-huu-tri-tue/Van-ban-hop-nhat-155-VBHN-VPQH-2025-Luat-So-huu-tri-tue-672556.aspx)). Published Vietnamese translations of Dickens-era novels remain copyrighted as derivatives even when the English is PD — do not scrape those. Do not MT copyrighted English (Newsela, TED, modern graded readers) and ship the output. Practical pattern: PD or exclusive-VOA English → human translation or MT plus **documented human revision** → provenance `public_domain` / `ATTRIBUTION_REQUIRED` on EN and `PRODUCTION_ALLOWED` on the human VI layer only.

StoryWeaver already hosts Vietnamese translations of English-origin stories and English translations of Vietnamese-origin stories under the platform **CC BY 4.0** book license (exclude videos). A 2021 classroom write-up reported **229 Vietnamese stories** at four levels; that count is dated and was not re-audited against the live catalog ([GUAVA / StoryWeaver](https://guavamerica.org/extensive-reading-in-the-vietnamese-language-classroom-with-storyweaver/)). That is the only production-cleared **children’s** bilingual reader family in this inventory. There is still no open adult EN–VI graded-reader series from Oxford, Cambridge, or Pearson.

## MVP stack is three licensed pools plus original items

Ship **four content lanes**, not a 20-source importer. Lane 1 is **VOA Learning English exclusive text + MP3**, credited to `learningenglish.voanews.com`, wire-scrubbed, CEFR-tagged in-house (expect unofficial A2–B1). That is the news-reading spine and the only high-volume learner-written commercial-reprint corpus in this research. Lane 2 is **StoryWeaver (and optionally Book Dash) CC BY 4.0 book text** — not read-alongs — with per-story author / illustrator / translator / publisher / donor attribution, used as A1–A2 extensive reading for Vietnamese beginners after cultural screening. Lane 3 is a **small dual-PD literature set**: Standard Ebooks HTML preferred as the file, Gutenberg `pg_catalog.csv` as the discovery catalog, author death year ≤ 1975, PG trademark stripped from any paid UI, optional LibriVox chapter audio for the same work, sliced to 300–800 words. Lane 4 is **EnglishFlow original**: every comprehension item, every CEFR label, adult A2–C1 passages that children’s books and 19th-century prose cannot supply, and human Vietnamese glosses or translations. Reuse existing **CEFR-J** as the leveling taxonomy (cite it; do not pretend it is a reader dump) and **Tatoeba / TALPCo** as sentence glosses under the passage, already mapped in grammar `content_sources`.

Hold Wikimedia / SQuAD / XQuAD / Belebele eval / FairytaleQA for a second wave if the product can carry ShareAlike (or Apache notice) on those rows. Wikivoyage is the best Wikimedia *passage* shape (travel guides) if SA is acceptable. Do not import OneStopEnglish, CLEAR, CEFR-SP, UniversalCEFR, EVBCorpus, PhoMT, MTet, TED, OpenSubtitles, SGK, or any Kaggle “CC0 ESL” scrape. Do not build a “questions-only” pipeline from RACE or ReClor. Do not bulk-crawl `www.gutenberg.org`; use mirrors and official feeds ([PG terms](https://www.gutenberg.org/policy/terms_of_use.html); [PG feeds](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/feeds/)).

| Priority | What to seed | `content_sources.license_code` | What EnglishFlow writes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | VOA LE exclusive articles + audio | `ATTRIBUTION_REQUIRED` | CEFR tag, items, optional human VI, wire scrub |
| 2 | StoryWeaver / Book Dash book text | `ATTRIBUTION_REQUIRED` | CEFR tag, items, keep platform attribution |
| 3 | Gutenberg / SE works cleared US **and** VN | `PRODUCTION_ALLOWED` | Slice, CEFR tag, items, optional human VI, optional LibriVox |
| 4 | Original adult passages + all quizzes | `PRODUCTION_ALLOWED` | The product differentiator |
| Support | CEFR-J taxonomy; Tatoeba / TALPCo glosses | `ATTRIBUTION_REQUIRED` | Same attribution pattern as grammar |
| Later | Wikivoyage / Simple WP; XQuAD / Belebele eval; FairytaleQA | `ATTRIBUTION_REQUIRED` (+ SA or Apache) | Only after SA product design |

Uncertainties that remain after this pass: Simple Wikipedia’s local GFDL-only page versus Foundation ToU (treat Foundation as controlling, confirm live banners before ship); territorial copyright of U.S. government works outside the U.S. (VOA LE’s own “commercial purposes, with credit” sentence is the operational grant for that corpus); whether embedding a CC BY-SA item in a closed lesson JSON is “Adapted Material”; CosmosQA / QuAC / DROP / MCTest official pages unread (`UNKNOWN`); live English-title counts on StoryWeaver and GDL; whether individual VOA lesson-page quizzes are also PD. None of those gaps blocks the four-lane MVP.

## Conclusion

The reading market’s pedagogical gold standard and its legal gold standard do not overlap. Newsela, RACE, British Council, and Breaking News English exist because they are good for learners; they are unusable because that quality is exactly what the rightsholders fence. EnglishFlow’s grammar precedent — cite CEFR-J, attribute Tatoeba, write theory in-house — still holds, but the *objects* change: grammar needed a taxonomy and sentences; reading needs passages you can store, level, and quiz. The durable product advantage is therefore inverted from the research temptation: seed a short list of license-clean stems, and treat questions, CEFR, and Vietnamese as original work rather than as a dataset you hoped someone else had already opened.
